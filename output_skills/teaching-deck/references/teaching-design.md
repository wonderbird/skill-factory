# Teaching Design

Rules for the outline and the workshop part. These illustrate the principle;
adapt them to the audience and the venue.

## Storyline

A teaching talk follows this arc:

1. **Hook** (1–2 min): a problem, a short story or a question the audience
   recognises from their own work. It makes the talk relevant before it
   explains anything.
2. **Goals and map** (1 min): the learning goals and the sections ahead.
3. **Concept blocks**: one block per learning goal. Each block runs concept,
   then a concrete example, then a check: a question to the room, a quick
   prediction ("what happens if…?") or a show of hands.
4. **Recap** (1–2 min): one slide that restates each learning goal as a
   takeaway.
5. **Bridge** (1 min): what the audience does next. With a workshop, the
   bridge introduces its task. Without one, it gives a first step for Monday
   morning and pointers for further reading.
6. **Questions.**

When the topics are several related subjects, order them so that each one
answers a question the previous one raised. Name that question on the
transition slide.

## Timing

- Plan with a buffer of 10 % of the talk duration for questions and
  overruns.
- A text or statement slide takes about 1 minute; a diagram or worked example
  takes 2 to 3 minutes; a title or transition slide takes about 15 seconds.
- Put an interaction point (a check or a question) at least every 8 to 10
  minutes.
- Mark one or two sections as "cut if late" in the outline, so the speaker
  knows what to drop.

## Workshop part

Each exercise gets its own slide group:

- **Task slide**: the goal, the steps, the timebox, the expected result, and
  how participants work (alone, pairs, groups).
- **Material**: what participants need prepared before the session (accounts,
  installed tools, sample files). List it in the outline, so the user can
  send it ahead.
- **Debrief slide**: two or three questions that connect the result back to a
  learning goal.

Size the first exercise so that every participant succeeds within the
timebox. Put harder variants on an optional "for the fast ones" slide.
